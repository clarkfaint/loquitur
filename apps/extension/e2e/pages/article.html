The tide machine

The harbour keeps a clock of its own. At the top of the hour the water stands still, and the boats that were leaning one way lean the other. Nobody on the quay looks up; everyone already knows.

A tide is not a wave that travels. It is the whole basin rising and falling at once, twice a day, on a schedule set by a body far too large to see. What a sailor watches is the consequence, not the cause.

In the winter the light leaves early and the water keeps its colour last. The lamps along the mole come on in a row, one after another, and the reflections take longer to settle than the lamps themselves.
