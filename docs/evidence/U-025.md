## 3. Architecture notes

**Highlights are ranges, not markup.** A block renders as one element holding one text node, so a sentence and a word are two pairs of offsets into that node: `rangeForSentence` uses F§5.1's `sentence.charStart`/`charEnd` and `rangeForWord` adds the word's own sentence-relative offsets, both walking the element's text nodes so the arithmetic survives a block that ever gains a second node. The ranges are registered under the names `lectern-sentence` and `lectern-word` in the browser's `CSS.highlights`, which is what paints them: no word and no sentence is ever wrapped in an element, so the text a reader selects, copies or searches is exactly the text the model holds.

Measured in Chromium 158.0.8127.18 (headless shell) on the harness page, with the shipped `ReaderView` mounted:

| Fact | Measurement |
|---|---|
| `document.caretPositionFromPoint` | `function` (and `caretRangeFromPoint` is a `function` too) |
| `CSS.highlights` present | true |
| Rules parsed by the CSSOM | `::highlight(lectern-sentence) { background-color: var(--lectern-sentence); }` and `::highlight(lectern-word) { background-color: var(--lectern-word); }` |
| Token values resolved | `--lectern-sentence` = `#e6f0ee`, `--lectern-word` = `#a9d3ce` (U-023's own tokens) |
| Child nodes of a rendered block | 1 (one text node) |
| Pixels of the active word's box (38 × 21 = 798) carrying `--lectern-word` | 333 while the position is active, **0** when it is cleared |
| `scroll` events raised by one programmatic `window.scrollBy(0, 200)` | 1 (from `scrollY` 0 to 200) |

The last two lines are why two design decisions below exist: the paint measurement proves the injected rules really colour the text rather than merely registering a range, and the scroll measurement proves that a scroll the reader performs itself is indistinguishable from a reader's scroll *by event alone*.

**Auto-scroll.** The policy is arithmetic over an injected clock. The band is 30 %–60 % of the viewport height, the resting place is 45 %, and the returned number is a scroll **delta**: `delta = round(rect.top − 0.45 × viewportHeight)`, added to the page's scroll offset, so the line's top lands at 45 % — `rect.top − delta` is the line's new position. A line whose top is inside the band is left alone. `onUserScroll()` stamps the clock, and `target` answers `null` until `suspendMs` (4000) has passed since that stamp; a second scroll mid-suspension moves the stamp rather than racing it.

Measured on a 200-paragraph fixture (viewport 720 px, 401 sentences, `body.scrollHeight` 6842), stepping the active position a fifth of the document apart and reading the page 150 ms later:

| Step | Sentence | `scrollY` | Active line's top / viewport |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 40 | 402 | 0.45 |
| 2 | 120 | 1762 | 0.45 |
| 3 | 200 | 3122 | 0.45 |
| 4 | 280 | 4482 | 0.45 |
| 5 | 360 | 5842 | 0.45 |

Every step moved the page within 150 ms and landed the line at exactly the resting place, which also proves the reader's own scrolling does not suspend its own policy — a reader that could not tell its own scroll apart would have stopped following for four seconds after the first step. The suspension itself, measured on the same fixture:

| Moment | `scrollY` | Line's top / viewport |
|---|---|---|
| before a real wheel scroll of −900 px | 5842 | 0.45 |
| 100 ms after it | 4942 | — |
| 1.6 s after it | 4942 | 1.7 (below the viewport) |
| 4.7 s after it | 5842 | 0.45 |

So the reader's own scroll holds the page still for four seconds while the line is off-screen, and then the reader returns to the line by itself: there is no button to press and no timeout to wait for beyond F§10.1's four seconds.

**The two entry points into a click.** `positionFromPoint` asks the browser for the caret under a point (`caretPositionFromPoint`, falling back to `caretRangeFromPoint`), climbs to the enclosing `[data-block]`, converts the caret into an offset into that block's text through a `Range`'s own `toString().length`, and then resolves the offset to a sentence and a word. An offset in the whitespace between two sentences joins the earlier one, an offset between two words joins the word before it, and a point that is not over a block at all answers `null`. That is what makes a click on the middle of a word land on that word rather than on its neighbour.

## 4. Lectern design

- [DESIGN] The two highlight rules are injected once per document as a `<style id="lectern-reader-highlights">` element by the reader module, using U-023's own tokens, because `packages/ui-web/src/styles.css` is another unit's file and R6 freezes it while this contract lists no Patch scope for it. They are the reader's own presentation, they invent no colour, and they are removed from the diff the moment a contract may edit that stylesheet — see §7.
- [DESIGN] Furniture is dimmed with an inline `opacity` (0.55) for the same reason, and is marked `data-skipped="true"` so the click handler can leave it alone. F§5.4 keeps furniture in the model and leaves its omission to the player; the reader shows it for reference, never as something to play from.
- [DESIGN] A block is one element holding one text node, which the e2e spec asserts (one child node per `[data-block]`) rather than assumes; consecutive `list-item` blocks are wrapped together in one `<ul>`, and the kinds with no element of their own (a `caption`, and the three furniture kinds) render as a paragraph. Headings take F§5.1's level, quotes take `<blockquote>`.
- [DESIGN] The list of blocks is computed once per document and per `showSkipped` and the component that draws it is memoised on that list, so a position change rewrites two ranges in an effect and rebuilds nothing. The delivered e2e case counts the reader's elements at every one of 50 position changes and requires a single distinct count, and requires the word highlight to have moved — a render that never committed would otherwise pass the count and prove nothing.
- [DESIGN] The reader tells its own scrolls apart from the reader's by remembering the `scrollY` the last programmatic scroll landed on (measured: one `scroll` event per `scrollBy`). Without that memory the first follow would suspend the policy that performed it, and the page would freeze for four seconds every time the line moved.
- [DESIGN] Auto-scroll follows the **word's** range, not the sentence's, because that is the line being read; a clamped scroll (the document's own end) is not remembered, so a page that cannot move cannot be mistaken for a reader's scroll.
- [DESIGN] The caret lookup prefers the standard `caretPositionFromPoint` and falls back to the legacy `caretRangeFromPoint`; both are present in this Chromium (measured), and the fallback costs one branch.
- [DESIGN] The harness gained four reader entry points — `mountReader(documentJson, { showSkipped })`, `setReaderActive(position)`, `readerSeeked()`, `readerDocumentJson(text)` — and mounts the reader in a `#reader` container. The contract names `mountReader`; the other three exist because a React commit, a registered highlight range and a caret position cannot be observed across the `page.evaluate` boundary otherwise, exactly as U-024's four sink entry points exist for its own assertions. `setReaderActive` commits inside `flushSync`, so a position change is on the page when the call returns.
- [DESIGN] The fixture text is Lectern's own, written for this unit; the harness turns it into a `DocumentModel` with the shipped `buildDocument`, so the e2e cases point at words by the indexes the model gives them rather than at hard-coded offsets.

## 7. Deviation proposals

- **The reader's own stylesheet rules live in the module because `styles.css` is frozen.** `::highlight(lectern-sentence)` and `::highlight(lectern-word)` — and the dimming of furniture — belong in `packages/ui-web/src/styles.css`, which R6 freezes for this unit and which the contract does not list under Patch scope; without them the registry would hold ranges that paint nothing. A contract that may edit that file should move the two rules there and drop `ensureHighlightRules`; a patch to this unit should say which of the two is authoritative.
- **`target(rect, viewportHeight): number | null` does not say whether the number is a delta or an absolute scroll position.** It is implemented as a delta (the line's new position is `rect.top − delta`), because a policy with no access to the scroll offset cannot express an absolute one; §3 states the arithmetic and the unit tests assert `rect.top − delta`.
- **The band is judged on the line's top edge**, so `target` reads only `rect.top`; the parameter type is `{ top: number }` so that a `DOMRect` from `getBoundingClientRect()` is accepted unchanged. If the intent is the line's centre or its whole box, the contract should say so.
- **F§10.1's "pauses for 4 s after manual scrolling" is silent on how a programmatic scroll is told from a manual one**, and on the web the two raise the same event (measured: one `scroll` event per `scrollBy`). The reader remembers the offset it asked for; a sentence in F§10.1 would make that an architecture decision rather than an implementation one.
- **A click on furniture seeks nowhere.** The contract's Forbidden Actions say skipped blocks must not be rendered as playable, so a dimmed furniture block is shown for reference and is inert; if a reader should be able to start from a footnote, that is a contract change.
- **The harness carries four reader entry points, not one.** `setReaderActive`, `readerSeeked` and `readerDocumentJson` are additions beside the contract's `mountReader`, for the same reason U-024's `createSink` and `makeChunk` are additions to its own two: the contract's own spec assertions cannot be made without them. A future contract could name all four.
- **The e2e spec carries a fourth case beyond the contract's three** (each block kind as its element, and furniture dimmed or hidden), because "semantic elements with `data-block`" and "do not render skipped blocks as playable" are requirements the contract states and no listed case would have checked.
