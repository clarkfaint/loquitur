## 3. Architecture notes

**One source, nine artefacts.** `source/lighthouse.md` is parsed once into a small in-memory shape (title,
three chapters with their paragraphs, the three-item list, the block quote, the footnote) and every other
file is *derived* from it, which is what the contract means by "builds every file below from
`source/lighthouse.md`" — no artefact is hand-written a second time, so the fixtures cannot drift apart.
`sample.md` is the source verbatim and `sample.txt` is the same document re-rendered from the parse with
inline markers stripped: the two differ in exactly the `*not*` emphasis, which is what makes the
inline-stripping rule of F§5.4 visible in a diff.

**Page geometry of `text.pdf`.** Letter, 612 × 792 points. F§5.4's bands are fractions of the page, so the
top band is `y ≥ 792 − 0.08·792 = 728.64` and the bottom band is `y ≤ 63.36` (y measured from the page
bottom, as pdf-lib draws). The running header is drawn at `y = 750` and the running footer at `y = 36`,
both inside their bands; the body column runs from `700` down to `110`, so no body line is ever inside a
band and the footnote — 10 pt at `y = 84`, below a 0.7 pt rule at `y = 100` — is at the foot of the page
but *outside* the bottom band, where it can never be mistaken for a second footer. Five pages carry the
same header text and a `Page N` footer, so each appears on 5/5 pages: `5 × 10 ≥ 5 × 6` holds (the integer
form of "at least 60%") and the "minimum 3 pages" clause is cleared with room to spare. Digit substitution
collapses `Page 1` … `Page 5` into the single key `Page #`, which is the only reason the footer is
detectable as *one* repeated line.

**The two font ratios are chosen so both F§5.4 tests decide.** Body text is 14 pt, so a chapter heading at
21 pt is exactly 1.5× the body size (the contract's wording) *and* clears "≥ 1.25× the page median" because
the median of a page dominated by body lines is the body size. The footnote is 10 pt: 0.714× the body,
below both "smaller font" and "0.85× the page median" (`10 < 11.9`). The furniture is 10 pt, which is
deliberately a size of its own, so a page's median cannot be dragged onto a furniture size while body lines
outnumber it.

**The setting is a double-spaced manuscript**, 14 pt type on 30 pt leading behind two-inch margins. That is
a plausible way to set this text *and* the one that makes the fixture exactly five pages: a capacity-driven
flow at manuscript spacing needs 4.1 pages for 719 words at the first attempt (12 pt / 28 pt leading) and
5.0 pages at the chosen spacing, so the page count is a consequence of the setting rather than a page-count
target bending the text. Pagination is ordinary flow — a line that would cross `y = 110` starts the next
page — and the resulting count is read back from the written file and asserted, not assumed.

**The hyphenated break is produced by real line breaking, not by a literal hyphen in the text.** The wrapper
measures with `widthOfTextAtSize` and, when a word does not fit the remaining column, breaks it at the last
fitting hyphenation point of a small compound dictionary. For `lighthouse` that means the line carries
`light-` and the next line begins `house`, which is precisely the shape F§5.4's de-hyphenation rule acts on;
the joined word is recorded in `expected.json`. Because the window is only ~34 pt wide (section 2), the
source phrasing was chosen by measurement rather than by taste, and `--check` fails if the break or the
lowercase continuation ever disappears.

**`scanned.pdf` is an image and nothing else.** Two pages, each a full-page `drawImage` of a canvas render
(Letter at 200 DPI, 1700 × 2200, matching F§5.5's rasterization resolution), so the pages extract **zero**
characters, the "< 20 extracted characters" rule fires, and no `PdfAnchor` can exist. `page.png` is the
other regime — A4 at 150 DPI, 1240 × 1754, 28 px sans-serif — so the two image fixtures cannot be confused.

**EPUB and DOCX are ZIP containers written by `fflate`** with a fixed modification stamp, so the committed
bytes are reproducible on any host and in any order. The EPUB's first entry is `mimetype`, stored
uncompressed, followed by `META-INF/container.xml`; the OPF's spine lists exactly the three chapter
documents, while `nav.xhtml` and the cover page exist in the manifest so that F§5.4's "`nav` and cover
documents are dropped" rule has something to drop. The DOCX carries `[Content_Types].xml`, the two
relationship parts, `word/styles.xml` (so `Title`, `Heading1` and `Heading2` are real styles rather than
direct formatting) and `word/numbering.xml` (so the three items are a real numbered list that `mammoth` maps
to a list instead of three flat paragraphs).

**Everything is read back, not trusted.** `--check` builds the whole set into a temporary directory, then
*inspects the written files*: it inflates the PDF's content streams and decodes the hex glyph runs, reads
the PNG's IHDR and counts ink through `loadImage`, opens both ZIP containers and parses the OPF and the
document part. Only content streams are searched for text (a stream whose bytes are not ≥ 95% printable
ASCII is an embedded image, and is skipped), so an image can never be mistaken for a text layer.

**Two builds must agree.** `--check` builds the artefacts twice and compares them byte for byte, so the
reproducibility this unit had to fix once (pdf-lib's clock-stamped dates) cannot silently return.

## 4. Lectern design

- [DESIGN] `source/lighthouse.md` is the single input; `generate.mjs` parses it once and renders all nine
  artefacts from that parse — follows the contract's `generate.mjs` bullet.
- [DESIGN] The footnote in the Markdown is a trailing paragraph whose text begins with the marker `1. `, and
  the document's *three-item list* is the `-` list inside chapter two. The Markdown reader (F§5.4) reads a
  `1.`-style line as a list item, so the marker is deliberately the F§5.4 footnote form
  (`^\d{1,3}[.)]?\s`) rather than a bullet that would merge the footnote into the chapter's list — follows
  F§5.4's footnote rule and the contract's "a three-item list … and one footnote".
- [DESIGN] `expected.json` carries exactly the contract's eight fields, defined as: `title` = the `#` title;
  `chapterTitles` = the three `##` headings in order; `firstSentenceOfChapter` = the first sentence of the
  first body paragraph *after* each chapter heading (not the heading itself); `headerText` = the running
  header drawn on every page; `footerPattern` = the footer with digits replaced by `#` (so `Page 1` and
  `Page 5` are one key); `footnoteStart` = the literal marker the footnote line begins with; `dehyphenatedWord`
  = the word the hyphenated break must yield with the hyphen removed; `pageImageWords` = the words actually
  drawn into `page.png`, in reading order (181 of them).
- [DESIGN] `--check` performs every validation the contract names (magic bytes, non-zero sizes, PDF page
  counts, exit 0/1) **and** the ones this unit's own artefacts make possible and the contract's *Forbidden
  Actions* imply: the header/footer really is in `text.pdf`'s inflated text layer on all five pages; the
  three font sizes are the ones the ratios require and the band arithmetic holds; `scanned.pdf` carries no
  text stream at all; `page.png`'s IHDR really is 1240 × 1754 with ink on it; the EPUB's `mimetype` is first
  and stored and its spine is three chapters with a nav and a cover; the DOCX has a title, exactly two
  headings and three genuinely numbered items; the two text artefacts differ exactly in the inline markers;
  `expected.json` agrees with the source and with the image; and two builds are byte-identical. It builds
  into a temporary directory and never writes the committed tree in check mode.
- [DESIGN] The generator validates its own source shape before doing anything (`sourceProblems`) and fails
  with a list of problems and exit 1 in both modes, because a source that cannot yield every artefact should
  read as a diagnosis, not as a stack trace — the modes were exercised on a deliberately broken copy
  (section 2).
- [DESIGN] Every ZIP entry is written with one fixed modification stamp (1980-01-01Z) because `fflate`
  refuses `mtime: 0` (measured), and both PDFs get the same instant through `setCreationDate` /
  `setModificationDate`, because pdf-lib otherwise stamps them from the clock (measured). A committed binary
  fixture is only reviewable if re-running the generator is a no-op.
- [DESIGN] `text.pdf` is drawn with the standard PDF fonts only (`StandardFonts.Helvetica` /
  `HelveticaBold`), which every PDF reader already has and which require no font file to be downloaded or
  embedded (contract: "Do not download fonts"; AGENTS.md §4.4: never embed reference assets).
- [DESIGN] `make-icons.mjs` draws the mark from primitives only — a rounded square plus three horizontal
  bars of decreasing length — using `roundRect` and a fresh canvas per size, so the set is genuinely
  type-free and never depends on which fonts the host exposes.
- [DESIGN] Only the three contract-named packages are installed, at exact versions (`pnpm add -E`):
  `pdf-lib`, `@napi-rs/canvas`, `fflate`. `@napi-rs/canvas` itself resolves a platform binary package
  (`@napi-rs/canvas-linux-x64-gnu`) — the named package's own optional dependency, not an additional
  Lectern dependency (R10).
- [DESIGN] Both scripts are plain Node ESM with no build step and no TypeScript, because the contract lists
  them as `.mjs` outputs and R5 allows no extra file (no `tsconfig.json`, no barrel) in this package — so
  the package declares no `typecheck` script and the standing `turbo run typecheck` gate stays scoped to
  `@lectern/core`.

## 7. Deviation proposals

- [DESIGN → contract] The contract requires "one word hyphenated across a line break in the PDF layout" but
  fixes no column width, and section 2 shows the break exists only inside a ~34 pt window of preceding text.
  This unit makes it deterministic by measuring the source phrasing against the column and asserting the
  break; a future edit to `lighthouse.md` that moves the word will fail `--check` rather than silently
  dropping the feature. Proposal: state the column width (or the requirement that `--check` assert the
  break), so the constraint is visible to whoever edits the fixture next.
- [DESIGN → contract] The contract's `--check` bullet requires only magic bytes, sizes and PDF page counts.
  Those are satisfiable by a file of the right *shape* and the wrong *content* — a 5-page PDF of nothing
  would pass. The extra validations in section 4 close that gap and cost nothing to run. Proposal: fold
  them into the contract so the next unit to touch fixtures is held to the same bar.
- [DESIGN → contract] **A fixed command can reach a step this unit may not fix.** `pnpm` (12.18.0) refuses
  the pre-script dependency verification for `@napi-rs/canvas@1.1.1` under its `minimumReleaseAge` policy and
  wants to record the exemption in `pnpm-workspace.yaml` — a path §5 makes read-only and this unit's scope
  excludes. Reverting it (as §5 prescribes) keeps the verification green but leaves every `pnpm` invocation
  printing `ERR_PNPM_MINIMUM_RELEASE_AGE_VIOLATION` and a `[WARN] … install … failed`. Proposal: either add
  `pnpm-workspace.yaml` to the always-allowed set, or record the policy decision once (`minimumReleaseAge`
  relaxed for this workspace, or `verifyDepsBeforeRun: false`) so the noise stops being re-derived by every
  unit that installs a package. No workaround was applied from inside the unit.
- [DESIGN → contract] `scanned.pdf` is rendered at 1700 × 2200 (Letter at 200 DPI) because F§5.5 names
  200 DPI as the rasterization resolution for the scanned-PDF fallback; the contract names no resolution.
  Proposal: record 200 DPI, so `page.png` (1240 × 1754, A4 at 150 DPI) and `scanned.pdf` are visibly two
  different regimes rather than two arbitrary sizes.
- [DESIGN → operator] `@napi-rs/canvas` adds four platform binary packages to `pnpm-lock.yaml` (linux x64
  gnu/musl, win32 x64/arm64). They are the named package's own optional dependencies, so R10 is untouched,
  but the operator should expect them in the lockfile diff — and, per the third proposal above, expect the
  policy warning that comes with them.
